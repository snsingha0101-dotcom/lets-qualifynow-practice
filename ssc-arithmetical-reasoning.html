Arithmetical Reasoning

Learn how to use basic arithmetic concepts, logical relationships and numerical information to solve reasoning-based problems quickly and accurately.

📘 What is Arithmetical Reasoning?

Arithmetical Reasoning is a reasoning topic in which numerical information is presented through a situation, statement or logical relationship.

The question may involve numbers, quantities, ages, operations, comparisons, ratios or other arithmetic relationships. The main task is to understand the given information and apply the correct reasoning step.

Basic Concept:

Understand the Situation → Identify the Relationship → Apply Arithmetic → Find the Answer

📚 Important Areas of Arithmetical Reasoning

1. Number-Based Problems

Questions involving numerical relationships and comparisons.

2. Age-Based Reasoning

Problems involving present age, past age and future age.

3. Quantity-Based Reasoning

Questions involving quantities, totals and differences.

4. Ratio-Based Reasoning

Questions where quantities are compared using ratios.

5. Arithmetic Operation Problems

Problems requiring addition, subtraction, multiplication or division according to the given conditions.

6. Logical Numerical Relationships

Questions in which a numerical relationship must be identified before performing the calculation.

➕ Basic Arithmetic Operations

Before solving Arithmetical Reasoning questions, be comfortable with the four basic operations.

Addition: a + b

Subtraction: a − b

Multiplication: a × b

Division: a ÷ b
Important: Identify what the numbers represent before performing an operation.

👨‍👩‍👧 Age-Based Arithmetical Reasoning

Age questions require careful understanding of the relationship between present, past and future ages.

Future Age = Present Age + Number of Years

Past Age = Present Age − Number of Years
Example

Ravi is 18 years old. What will be his age after 7 years?

Solution: 18 + 7 = 25 years

⚖️ Ratio-Based Reasoning

A ratio compares two quantities of the same type.

Ratio of A to B = A : B
Example

The ratio of boys to girls in a group is 2 : 3. If there are 10 boys, how many girls are there?

Solution:
2 parts = 10
1 part = 5
3 parts = 15

Answer: 15 girls

🔢 Difference and Comparison

Many reasoning questions ask you to compare two quantities.

Difference = Larger Quantity − Smaller Quantity
Example

A box contains 48 items and another box contains 31 items. Find the difference.

Solution: 48 − 31 = 17

➕ Total and Combined Quantity

When separate quantities are combined, addition is generally used.

Example

A student solves 24 questions in one practice set and 18 questions in another. How many questions were solved in total?

Solution: 24 + 18 = 42 questions

🧠 Understanding Word-Based Problems

In Arithmetical Reasoning, the calculation is often hidden inside a sentence. The first task is to convert the language into a mathematical relationship.

✍️ SSC-Level Solved Examples

Example 1

A number is 17 more than 28. Find the number.

Solution: 28 + 17 = 45
Example 2

A number is 15 less than 62. Find the number.

Solution: 62 − 15 = 47
Example 3

The sum of two numbers is 72. One number is 29. Find the other number.

Solution: 72 − 29 = 43
Example 4

A shop has 7 boxes. Each box contains 12 books. Find the total number of books.

Solution: 7 × 12 = 84 books
Example 5

96 students are divided equally into 8 groups. How many students are there in each group?

Solution: 96 ÷ 8 = 12 students
Example 6

A father is 42 years old and his son is 14 years old. Find the difference between their ages.

Solution: 42 − 14 = 28 years

🔗 Identifying Numerical Relationships

Some questions require you to understand the relationship before calculating.

Example

If 5 notebooks cost ₹100, what is the cost of one notebook, assuming the price is the same for each notebook?

Solution: ₹100 ÷ 5 = ₹20
Exam Tip: First identify whether the problem is asking for a total, difference, equal share, multiple or comparison.

🔢 Arithmetic Relationships in Sequences

Numerical relationships can also appear in a sequence. Check the difference or multiplication pattern before deciding the rule.

Example

Find the next number:

8, 13, 18, 23, ?

Solution: Each number increases by 5.

23 + 5 = 28

➗ Arithmetic Operations and BODMAS

When multiple arithmetic operations appear together, the order of operations must be followed correctly.

B = Brackets
O = Orders / Of
D = Division
M = Multiplication
A = Addition
S = Subtraction
Example

Solve: 12 + 6 × 2

Solution: First multiplication:
6 × 2 = 12
12 + 12 = 24

📝 Step-by-Step SSC Solving Method

  1. Read the complete question carefully.
  2. Identify all numerical information.
  3. Identify exactly what is being asked.
  4. Translate the wording into a mathematical relationship.
  5. Select the required arithmetic operation.
  6. Perform the calculation carefully.
  7. Check whether the result is reasonable.
  8. Match the answer with the options.
Quick Formula:

Read → Identify → Relate → Calculate → Verify → Answer

❌ Common Mistakes

1. Performing the Wrong Operation

Do not add when the question requires subtraction or division.

2. Ignoring the Meaning of the Question

Understand what the numbers represent before calculating.

3. Wrong Order of Operations

Follow BODMAS whenever multiple operations are present.

4. Arithmetic Calculation Errors

Recheck simple calculations when time permits.

5. Ignoring Units

Keep track of whether the answer is in years, books, rupees, students or another unit.
