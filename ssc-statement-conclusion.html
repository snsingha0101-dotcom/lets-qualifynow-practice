Statement & Conclusion

Learn how to identify logically valid conclusions from a given statement using a clear and exam-oriented approach for SSC Reasoning.

📘 What is Statement & Conclusion?

In Statement & Conclusion questions, a statement is given followed by one or more conclusions. Your task is to determine whether the given conclusion logically follows from the statement.

Important: A conclusion must be based only on the information given in the statement. Do not use outside knowledge, personal opinion or assumptions.

These questions mainly test your ability to understand information, identify logical consequences and distinguish between what is actually stated and what is merely possible or assumed.

🔍 Statement vs Conclusion

Statement: The information, fact, situation or condition given in the question.
Conclusion: A logical inference that necessarily follows from the statement.

Example

Statement:

All students who regularly practise reasoning questions improve their problem-solving speed.

Conclusion:

Regular reasoning practice can improve problem-solving speed.

✓ Conclusion follows.
The conclusion directly reflects the relationship given in the statement.

🎯 Golden Rule of Statement & Conclusion

Only use the information contained in the statement.
  • The conclusion must logically follow from the statement.
  • Do not add any new information.
  • Do not use general knowledge unless it is explicitly given.
  • Do not make a conclusion merely because it seems possible.
  • A conclusion should be supported by the statement.
  • Read the statement carefully before judging the conclusion.

📚 Important Types of Conclusions

1. Direct Conclusion

The conclusion directly follows from the information given in the statement.

2. Logical / Inference-Based Conclusion

The conclusion is not written in exactly the same words, but it can be logically derived from the statement.

3. Positive Conclusion

The conclusion establishes or supports something that follows from the statement.

4. Negative Conclusion

The conclusion indicates that something does not follow or is not supported by the given information.

Remember: The wording may change, but the logical relationship must remain supported by the statement.

🧠 How to Identify a Correct Conclusion

1

Read the Statement

Understand exactly what information has been provided.

2

Read the Conclusion

Check what the conclusion is trying to establish.

3

Connect the Information

Ask whether the conclusion is directly supported by the statement.

4

Remove Outside Knowledge

Judge the conclusion only on the basis of the given statement.

5

Check Necessity

Ask: "Does this conclusion necessarily follow?"

6

Choose the Answer

Select the conclusion that is logically supported.

✍️ SSC-Level Solved Examples

Example 1

Statement:

All employees who complete the training programme receive a certificate.

Conclusion:

Employees who complete the training programme receive a certificate.

✓ Follows
The conclusion is directly supported by the statement.
Example 2

Statement:

The library remains open until 8 PM on weekdays.

Conclusion:

The library remains open until 8 PM on every day of the week.

✗ Does Not Follow
The statement specifically mentions weekdays. It gives no information about weekends.
Example 3

Statement:

A company introduced an online application system to reduce paperwork.

Conclusion:

The company wanted to reduce paperwork.

✓ Follows
The purpose of introducing the system is explicitly stated.
Example 4

Statement:

Many candidates attend mock tests before the examination.

Conclusion:

Every candidate attends mock tests before the examination.

✗ Does Not Follow
"Many" does not mean "every". The conclusion makes the statement stronger than the information provided.
Example 5

Statement:

The school provides free books to students belonging to economically weaker sections.

Conclusion:

Students belonging to economically weaker sections can receive free books from the school.

✓ Follows
The conclusion is a direct logical consequence of the statement.
Example 6

Statement:

The city administration has announced a special bus service during the festival.

Conclusion:

The special bus service will operate throughout the year.

✗ Does Not Follow
The statement only refers to the festival period and does not establish year-round operation.

⚠️ Important Words to Watch

Certain words can completely change the strength of a conclusion. Pay special attention to them.

  • All — refers to the complete group.
  • Some — refers to at least a part of a group.
  • Many — does not mean all.
  • Only — creates a specific restriction.
  • Every — indicates the complete group.
  • Always — indicates all situations or occasions.
  • Never — indicates complete absence.
  • May — indicates possibility, not certainty.
Exam Tip: A conclusion that changes "some" into "all", "may" into "must", or "many" into "every" is usually not supported by the statement.

🚀 Quick Solving Method

  1. Read the statement carefully.
  2. Underline or mentally note the main facts.
  3. Read each conclusion separately.
  4. Check whether the conclusion is supported by the statement.
  5. Do not add assumptions.
  6. Check whether the conclusion is too broad or too strong.
  7. Select only the conclusion that logically follows.
Fast Test:
Ask yourself:

"Can I prove this conclusion using only the given statement?"

If yes, it may follow. If additional information is required, it does not follow.

❌ Common Mistakes

1. Using Outside Knowledge

Do not use facts that are not mentioned in the statement.

2. Confusing Possibility with Certainty

Something being possible does not necessarily mean that it follows as a conclusion.

3. Making the Conclusion Too Broad

"Some" cannot automatically become "all" or "every".

4. Assuming Additional Information

Do not add conditions, reasons or facts that are not provided.

5. Ignoring Exact Wording

Words such as all, some, many, only, may and must are important.
