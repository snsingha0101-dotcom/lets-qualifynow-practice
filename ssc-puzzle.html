🧩 Puzzle

Learn the concepts, clues, arrangement techniques and logical approach required to solve SSC-level Puzzle questions.

📘 What is Puzzle?

Puzzle is a type of reasoning question in which several pieces of information are given through different clues. You have to arrange, match or determine the information logically and answer the questions based on it.

Puzzle questions mainly test your ability to understand conditions, connect information and reach a logical conclusion.

Key Idea: Read every clue carefully, convert the information into a suitable table or arrangement and then use the clues one by one.

🔹 Important Types of Puzzles

1. Linear Puzzle

Persons or objects are arranged in a straight line according to given conditions.

2. Floor Puzzle

Persons or objects are placed on different floors of a building according to given clues.

3. Box Puzzle

Different objects or items are arranged in boxes or different positions.

4. Day / Month Puzzle

Persons, events or activities are assigned to different days or months.

5. Selection Puzzle

A group has to be selected according to several conditions and restrictions.

6. Distribution Puzzle

Items, persons or objects are distributed among different groups according to given conditions.

📝 Important Puzzle Terms

  • Before: One position or event comes earlier than another.
  • After: One position or event comes later than another.
  • Immediately Before: Directly one position before, with no position in between.
  • Immediately After: Directly one position after, with no position in between.
  • Adjacent: Directly next to a person or object.
  • Between: Positioned between two specified persons, objects or positions.
  • Above: Higher position in a floor-based arrangement.
  • Below: Lower position in a floor-based arrangement.
  • Either...or: One of the stated possibilities is applicable.
  • Neither...nor: Both stated possibilities are excluded.

🧠 Basic Approach to Puzzle Questions

  1. Read the complete question carefully.
  2. Identify what has to be arranged or determined.
  3. Separate fixed information from conditional information.
  4. Start with the strongest or most definite clue.
  5. Create a table, row, column or suitable diagram.
  6. Place the confirmed information first.
  7. Use the remaining clues one by one.
  8. Check every condition before answering.
Golden Rule: Never try to remember all clues mentally. Convert the information into a diagram or table whenever possible.

📊 Using a Table for Puzzles

A table makes the information easier to organize and reduces the possibility of missing an important condition.

Example:

Five persons A, B, C, D and E live on different floors from 1 to 5.

A lives above C.
B lives immediately below D.
E lives on the top floor.

Initial Table:

Floor 5 → E
Floor 4 → ?
Floor 3 → ?
Floor 2 → ?
Floor 1 → ?

Now use the remaining conditions to fill the positions.

Learning Point: Start with the fixed clue first. Here, E being on the top floor gives an immediate position.

✅ Solved Example 1 — Linear Puzzle

Five persons A, B, C, D and E sit in a row facing North.

A sits at the extreme left.
B sits immediately to the right of A.
C sits immediately to the right of B.
D sits at the extreme right.

Question: Who sits between C and D?

Arrangement:

A → B → C → E → D

Answer: E
E is positioned between C and D.

✅ Solved Example 2 — Order-Based Puzzle

Five students P, Q, R, S and T have different ranks.

P ranks above Q.
R ranks below S.
T ranks above P.
S ranks above T.

Question: Who ranks first?

The relations give:

S → T → P → Q

R is below S.

Answer: S
S is above T, P and Q and therefore occupies the highest position among the given relations.

✅ Solved Example 3 — Floor Puzzle

Four persons A, B, C and D live on floors 1 to 4.

A lives above B.
C lives on the top floor.
D lives immediately above B.

Since C is on floor 4, A, B and D occupy floors 1, 2 and 3.

D is immediately above B. Since A must also be above B, the only possible arrangement is:

Floor 4 → C
Floor 3 → A
Floor 2 → D
Floor 1 → B

Learning Point: In floor puzzles, first place the fixed-floor clue and then connect the immediate-position and above/below conditions.

✅ Solved Example 4 — Day Arrangement

Four subjects P, Q, R and S are studied from Monday to Thursday, one subject each day.

P is studied before Q.
R is studied immediately after P.
S is studied on Thursday.

Since S is Thursday, P-R must occupy Monday-Tuesday.

Therefore:

Monday → P
Tuesday → R
Wednesday → Q
Thursday → S

Answer: Q is studied on Wednesday.

🔍 Fixed Clues & Conditional Clues

Fixed Clues

A fixed clue directly gives a position or relationship.

Example: A is on the 5th floor.

Conditional Clues

A conditional clue depends on another position or condition.

Example: B sits immediately before C.
Strategy: Always try to use fixed clues first and then connect conditional clues to them.

⚠️ Negative Clues

Some puzzle questions contain restrictions such as:

  • A is not at the extreme end.
  • B does not sit next to C.
  • D is not on the first floor.
  • P is not scheduled on Monday.

Such clues do not directly give a position, but they eliminate impossible possibilities.

Important: Do not ignore negative clues. They are often used to eliminate one or more possible arrangements.

🎯 How to Solve Puzzle Questions

  1. Identify the number of persons, objects or positions.
  2. Identify the type of puzzle.
  3. Write the positions clearly.
  4. Place fixed clues first.
  5. Connect related clues.
  6. Use negative clues to eliminate possibilities.
  7. Complete the arrangement.
  8. Verify every clue before selecting the answer.

📝 SSC-Level Practice Questions

Q1.

Five persons A, B, C, D and E stand in a row. A is to the left of B. C is immediately to the right of B. D is at the extreme right and E is between C and D. Who stands in the middle?

Answer: C

Q2.

Four persons P, Q, R and S live on floors 1 to 4. S lives on the top floor. P lives above Q. R lives immediately above Q. Who lives on floor 2?

Answer: R

Q3.

Five subjects A, B, C, D and E are arranged from Monday to Friday. A is on Monday. C is immediately after A. E is on Friday. B is immediately before D. Which subject is on Wednesday?

Answer: B

Q4.

Six persons P, Q, R, S, T and U stand in a row. P is immediately before Q. R is immediately after Q. S is at the extreme left and U is at the extreme right. Who is between R and U?

Answer: T

Q5.

Five persons A, B, C, D and E live on different floors. E lives on floor 5. A lives immediately above B. C lives below B. D lives below C. Who lives on floor 1?

Answer: D

Q6.

Four activities P, Q, R and S are scheduled from Monday to Thursday. P is before Q. R is immediately after P. S is on Thursday. Which activity is on Wednesday?

Answer: Q

Q7.

Five persons A, B, C, D and E sit in a row. A sits immediately before B. C sits immediately after D. E sits at the extreme right. If A is at the first position, who sits at the third position?

Answer: D

Q8.

Five students P, Q, R, S and T have different ranks. S ranks above T. T ranks above P. P ranks above Q. R ranks below S. Who ranks highest?

Answer: S

❌ Common Mistakes

  • Trying to solve the entire puzzle mentally.
  • Ignoring a small condition.
  • Starting with a weak clue instead of a fixed clue.
  • Confusing immediately before/after with simply before/after.
  • Forgetting negative conditions.
  • Not checking the final arrangement against every clue.
  • Spending too much time on one uncertain possibility.

⚡ Quick Solving Method

  1. Identify the puzzle type.
  2. Draw the required positions.
  3. Place fixed information.
  4. Connect related clues.
  5. Eliminate impossible cases.
  6. Verify all conditions.
  7. Answer only after the arrangement is confirmed.
Exam Tip: The fastest solver is not the one who reads the clues fastest; it is the one who organizes the information correctly.

📌 Quick Revision

  • Puzzles are based on multiple interconnected clues.
  • Identify the puzzle type before solving.
  • Use fixed clues first.
  • Use tables and diagrams.
  • Pay attention to immediately, adjacent, before and after.
  • Negative clues eliminate possibilities.
  • Verify every condition before answering.
  • Practice improves speed and accuracy.
