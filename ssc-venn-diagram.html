📘 What is a Venn Diagram?

A Venn Diagram is a visual representation used to show the relationship between different groups, categories or sets.

In SSC Reasoning, Venn Diagram questions generally test your ability to understand the relationship between different categories.

🎯 SSC Focus: Identify whether groups are completely different, partially overlapping, completely included or related through common elements.

🔹 Basic Concept of Sets

A Set is a collection of similar or related objects.

Example

Set A: Students who study Mathematics
Set B: Students who study Reasoning

Some students may study both Mathematics and Reasoning. Therefore, the two sets may overlap.

Mathematics
Reasoning

📊 Important Types of Venn Diagrams

1️⃣ Completely Separate Sets

When two groups have no common element, they are represented separately.

Example: Dogs and Cars
Dogs
Cars

2️⃣ Overlapping Sets

When some elements are common to both groups, the circles overlap.

Example: Students who like Cricket and Football.
Cricket
Football

3️⃣ Completely Included Set

When one group is completely contained within another group, one circle is placed inside another.

Example: Men and Fathers
Men
Important: Every Father is a Man, but every Man is not necessarily a Father.

⭕ Two-Set Venn Diagram

In two-set questions, we generally have two categories and their common portion.

Example:

In a class, 30 students like Cricket, 20 like Football and 10 like both.

Formula

A ∪ B = A + B − (A ∩ B)

Here:

  • A = Number of elements in Set A
  • B = Number of elements in Set B
  • A ∩ B = Common elements
  • A ∪ B = Total elements in either A or B
Total = 30 + 20 − 10
Answer = 40 students

⭕ Three-Set Venn Diagram

Three-set questions involve three different categories and their common regions.

A
B
C
The central region represents elements common to all three sets.

Inclusion–Exclusion Formula

A ∪ B ∪ C = A + B + C − AB − BC − CA + ABC

This formula is especially useful in questions involving three overlapping groups.

🔵 Common / Overlapping Region

The overlapping portion of two or more circles represents the elements common to those groups.

Example:
Students who study both English and Reasoning belong to the common region of the two sets.
Remember: "Both A and B" means the intersection/common region.

🔑 Union and Intersection

Term Meaning
A ∪ B Elements belonging to A or B or both
A ∩ B Elements common to A and B
A − B Elements belonging to A but not B
B − A Elements belonging to B but not A

➕ Inclusion–Exclusion Principle

Inclusion–Exclusion is used when categories overlap and we need to find the total number of unique elements.

Two Sets

Total = A + B − Common

Three Sets

Total = A + B + C − AB − BC − CA + ABC
⚠️ Common elements are counted more than once during addition. Therefore, overlapping regions must be adjusted.

🟢 Nested Venn Diagram

A nested Venn Diagram represents a hierarchy where one category is completely contained inside another.

Example:
Animals → Mammals → Dogs
Every Dog is a Mammal and every Mammal is an Animal. Therefore, Dogs ⊂ Mammals ⊂ Animals.

📝 SSC-Level Solved Examples

Question 1:
Which diagram best represents the relationship between Doctors, Women and Humans?
Answer: Doctors and Women are two groups that can overlap, while both belong to the larger group of Humans.
Question 2:
Which diagram represents the relationship between Teachers, Men and Women?
Answer: Teachers can be both Men and Women, so the Teacher group can overlap with both categories.
Question 3:
Which diagram represents the relationship between Fruits, Apples and Mangoes?
Answer: Apples and Mangoes are types of Fruits. Both belong inside the larger Fruits category.
Question 4:
Which diagram represents the relationship between Animals, Dogs and Cats?
Answer: Dogs and Cats are both Animals, but they are separate categories.
Question 5:
In a group, 40 students like Tea, 30 like Coffee and 15 like both. How many students like at least one of the two?
Solution:
40 + 30 − 15 = 55

Answer: 55 students

🧠 How to Solve Venn Diagram Questions

  1. Read all the categories carefully.
  2. Identify whether the groups are separate or overlapping.
  3. Check whether one group is completely inside another.
  4. Identify common elements.
  5. For numerical questions, write the given values clearly.
  6. Use the appropriate formula.
  7. Do not count the common region twice.
  8. Check whether the question asks for total, common or separate elements.
⚡ Exam Tip: First understand the relationship between categories. Do not immediately start calculating.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Counting the common region twice.
  • Confusing Union with Intersection.
  • Ignoring the words "only", "both", "at least" and "neither".
  • Putting a category outside its parent category.
  • Assuming two categories are separate without checking the statement.
  • Using the two-set formula directly in every three-set question.
