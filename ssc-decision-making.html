Decision Making

Learn how to analyse situations, identify relevant information and select the most logical decision using exam-oriented reasoning.

📘 What is Decision Making?

Decision Making questions present a situation, problem or set of conditions. You have to analyse the available information and choose the most appropriate course of action.

The correct decision should be based on the facts, conditions and requirements given in the question rather than on personal preference.

Basic Concept:

Situation → Analyse Facts → Compare Options → Decision

🎯 Core Principle of Decision Making

A good decision should satisfy the important conditions mentioned in the problem.

Important Factors:
  • Facts
  • Rules or conditions
  • Available options
  • Consequences
  • Time or resource limitations
  • Priority of the problem
Remember:
The most attractive option is not necessarily the correct option. Choose the option that best satisfies the stated conditions.

📚 Important Types of Decision Making

1. Rule-Based Decision

A decision is made according to clearly stated rules or conditions.

Example:

An application is accepted only when the candidate satisfies all eligibility conditions.

2. Priority-Based Decision

When several options are available, the option satisfying the highest-priority requirement is considered first.

3. Resource-Based Decision

The decision depends on available time, money, manpower or other resources.

4. Situation-Based Decision

The correct action depends on the facts and circumstances of the given situation.

5. Consequence-Based Decision

The possible result of each option is considered before choosing an action.

🧠 How to Solve Decision Making Questions

1

Read the Situation

Understand exactly what problem or decision is being presented.

2

Identify the Conditions

Note every rule, restriction and requirement given in the question.

3

List the Options

Consider the possible courses of action presented.

4

Eliminate Invalid Options

Remove options that violate any important condition.

5

Compare Remaining Options

Check which option best satisfies the stated requirements.

6

Make the Decision

Select the option supported by the facts and conditions.

⭐ Rule-Based Decision Example

Example

A scholarship is available only to students who have scored at least 80% and have attendance of at least 75%.

Student A: Score = 84%, Attendance = 78%

Student B: Score = 86%, Attendance = 68%

Decision: Student A satisfies both conditions.
Why not Student B? Student B satisfies the score condition but fails the attendance condition.

🔢 Multiple Conditions

Many examination questions contain several conditions. A candidate or option may need to satisfy all of them.

Example Conditions:
  1. Age must be at least 18 years.
  2. Qualification must be graduation.
  3. Minimum percentage must be 60%.
If an option fails even one compulsory condition, it cannot be selected when the question requires all conditions to be satisfied.

⚖️ Priority-Based Decision

Sometimes more than one option appears reasonable. In such cases, identify the requirement that has the highest priority.

Example

A student has one hour available and needs to prepare for a test tomorrow. The student has not studied two important chapters.

The student should first focus on the chapters that are important for the test rather than spending the available time on unrelated activities.

Decision Principle: Address the most relevant and urgent requirement first.

💰 Resource-Based Decision

A decision may depend on limited resources such as time, money, manpower or available facilities.

Example

A team has a fixed budget and must choose one of two necessary improvements. One option fits the available budget while the other exceeds it.

Decision Principle: The option that satisfies the requirement without violating the stated resource limit can be considered feasible.

🔮 Considering Consequences

A decision should also be evaluated by considering its likely consequences within the information given in the question.

Example

A student has an examination in two days and must choose between revising the syllabus or starting an unrelated new topic.

Decision Principle: Revision of the examination syllabus directly addresses the immediate objective.

✍️ SSC-Level Solved Examples

Example 1

A training programme requires a minimum score of 70% in an entrance test. Ravi scored 76%.

Can Ravi satisfy the score requirement?

Answer: Yes. Ravi scored above the minimum required 70%.
Example 2

A position requires graduation and at least 60% marks. Aman is a graduate with 64% marks.

Answer: Aman satisfies both stated conditions.
Example 3

A course requires graduation and 60% marks. Neha is a graduate but has scored 54%.

Answer: Neha does not satisfy all the stated conditions because her percentage is below 60%.
Example 4

A student has limited preparation time. The examination is scheduled for the next day and several important topics remain unrevised.

Which should receive priority: revision of important topics or learning an unrelated new subject?

Answer: Revision of the important examination topics should receive priority because it directly addresses the immediate objective.
Example 5

A company has a fixed budget for a required safety improvement. Option A satisfies the safety requirement within the budget. Option B provides a similar improvement but exceeds the stated budget.

Answer: Option A is feasible under the stated conditions because it satisfies the requirement without exceeding the budget.
Example 6

A candidate satisfies the required educational qualification but does not satisfy the stated age condition.

Answer: The candidate does not satisfy all compulsory conditions.

📝 SSC-Level Practice Questions

Q1.

A scholarship requires at least 75% marks and 80% attendance. Rahul has 82% marks and 84% attendance.

Does Rahul satisfy both conditions?

Answer: Yes. Rahul satisfies both the marks and attendance conditions.
Q2.

A programme requires graduation and at least 60% marks. Priya is a graduate with 58% marks.

Does Priya satisfy all the conditions?

Answer: No. She does not satisfy the minimum percentage condition.
Q3.

An examination is scheduled for tomorrow. A student has not revised several important chapters.

Which should receive priority: revising the important chapters or starting an unrelated topic?

Answer: Revising the important chapters should receive priority because it directly supports the immediate examination objective.
Q4.

A project has a fixed budget. Option A meets the required specifications within the budget, while Option B exceeds the budget.

Which option is feasible under the stated budget condition?

Answer: Option A.
Q5.

A candidate satisfies the educational qualification but fails the compulsory age condition.

Can the candidate be considered eligible when both conditions are compulsory?

Answer: No. Failing one compulsory condition means all required conditions are not satisfied.
Q6.

A team must complete an urgent task today. Two options are available, but one requires resources that are not available.

Which option should be considered?

Answer: The feasible option that can be completed using the available resources should be considered.
Q7.

A candidate meets all the compulsory requirements for a selection process.

What should be checked next if the question provides additional conditions?

Answer: The additional stated conditions should also be checked before making the final decision.
Q8.

A student has limited time before an examination and two activities are available: revising an important topic or spending the same time on an unrelated activity.

Which action is more directly aligned with the immediate objective?

Answer: Revising the important topic.

❌ Common Mistakes

1. Ignoring a Condition

Never ignore a compulsory condition mentioned in the question.

2. Choosing Based on Personal Preference

Select according to the facts and conditions, not personal likes or dislikes.

3. Selecting the Most Attractive Option

The most attractive option may still violate an important condition.

4. Forgetting Resource Limits

Time, money and other stated limitations must be considered.

5. Jumping to the Final Decision

First analyse all relevant conditions and eliminate invalid options.

⚡ Quick Solving Method

  1. Read the situation carefully.
  2. Identify the objective.
  3. Mark all compulsory conditions.
  4. Check each option against the conditions.
  5. Eliminate options that violate any compulsory rule.
  6. Compare the remaining feasible options.
  7. Select the option that best satisfies the stated objective.
Quick Formula:

Facts → Conditions → Elimination → Comparison → Decision
