Cause & Effect

Understand the relationship between an event and its possible cause or effect with clear SSC-oriented reasoning concepts.

📘 What is Cause & Effect?

Cause & Effect questions are based on the relationship between two events or situations.

A cause is an event, condition or situation that produces or contributes to another event.

An effect is the result or consequence that occurs because of a particular cause.

Basic Concept:

Cause → Event/Condition → Effect

In examination questions, you may be asked to identify which statement represents the cause, which represents the effect, or whether two statements are related through cause and effect.

🔍 Cause vs Effect

Cause

The reason or condition that leads to an event.

Effect

The result or consequence produced by an event or condition.

Simple Example

Heavy rainfall occurred.

Several roads were flooded.

Cause: Heavy rainfall
Effect: Several roads were flooded

🧠 How to Identify Cause and Effect

1

Find the Event

Identify what happened or what situation is being described.

2

Ask "Why?"

Ask what could reasonably have caused the event.

3

Ask "What Happened Because of It?"

Identify the possible consequence or result.

4

Check the Direction

Cause generally occurs before the effect in the logical sequence.

5

Check the Relationship

Make sure the two events have a meaningful connection.

6

Avoid Mere Sequence

Two events occurring one after another do not automatically mean that one caused the other.

⭐ Most Important Rule

Sequence ≠ Cause & Effect

Just because Event A happened before Event B does not prove that Event A caused Event B.

The relationship must be logical and reasonably connected.

Example

Event A: A student bought a new pen.

Event B: The student's examination started.

Although Event A may occur before Event B, buying a new pen does not necessarily cause the examination to start.

Conclusion: Mere chronological order is not sufficient to establish a cause-effect relationship.

📚 Important Types of Cause & Effect

1. Direct Cause

One event directly produces another event.

Example:

The glass fell from the table and broke.

Falling of the glass is the direct cause of it breaking.

2. Natural Cause

A natural event produces a particular consequence.

Example:

Heavy snowfall caused roads to become difficult to travel on.

3. Human-Created Cause

Human action or decision leads to a consequence.

Example:

Poor maintenance resulted in frequent equipment failures.

4. Multiple Causes

More than one factor may contribute to an event.

Example:

Traffic congestion may be caused by road construction, increased vehicle volume and poor traffic management.

5. Multiple Effects

One cause can produce several consequences.

Example:

A major power failure may stop machines, interrupt communication and delay operations.

🔗 Important Cause & Effect Clues

Certain words often indicate a cause-effect relationship.

  • because
  • therefore
  • due to
  • as a result
  • hence
  • consequently
  • resulted in
  • led to
  • caused
  • owing to
Important: These words are clues, not automatic proof. Always check whether the relationship is logically meaningful.

✍️ SSC-Level Solved Examples

Example 1

Statement I: Heavy rain continued throughout the night.

Statement II: Several low-lying areas were flooded.

Cause: Heavy rain continued throughout the night.
Effect: Several low-lying areas were flooded.
The statements have a logical cause-effect relationship.
Example 2

Statement I: The company introduced additional safety training.

Statement II: Employees became more familiar with workplace safety procedures.

Cause: Additional safety training.
Effect: Employees became more familiar with safety procedures.
Example 3

Statement I: The road was closed for repair work.

Statement II: Vehicles had to use an alternative route.

Cause: Road closure for repair work.
Effect: Vehicles had to use an alternative route.
Example 4

Statement I: A student did not revise the topics before the examination.

Statement II: The student found it difficult to recall several concepts.

Cause: Lack of revision.
Effect: Difficulty in recalling concepts.
Example 5

Statement I: A shop opened at 8:00 AM.

Statement II: A customer arrived at the shop at 8:10 AM.

Conclusion: The first statement does not necessarily cause the second. The events may simply occur in sequence.
Example 6

Statement I: The city experienced a long power outage.

Statement II: Several businesses temporarily stopped their operations.

Cause: Long power outage.
Effect: Several businesses temporarily stopped their operations.

🔄 Cause, Effect & Sequence

Cause: The event that produces or contributes to another event.
Effect: The result or consequence of another event.
Sequence: Two events simply occurring one after another without a proven causal connection.
Exam Tip: Always ask:

"Did the first event reasonably lead to the second?"

❌ Common Mistakes

1. Confusing Sequence with Cause

Just because one event happened first does not mean it caused the next event.

2. Ignoring Logical Connection

Both statements must have a meaningful relationship.

3. Assuming Every Result is Certain

Use the information and reasonable logic given in the question.

4. Confusing Correlation with Direct Cause

Two events may occur together without one directly causing the other.

5. Ignoring Time Direction

A cause generally precedes its effect in a logical sequence.

⚡ Quick Solving Method

  1. Read both statements carefully.
  2. Identify the main event in each statement.
  3. Ask which event could reasonably produce the other.
  4. Check whether the relationship is logical.
  5. Check whether it is more than mere sequence.
  6. Mark the cause and effect accordingly.
Quick Formula:

Why did it happen? → Cause
What happened because of it? → Effect
