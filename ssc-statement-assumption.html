Statement & Assumption

Learn how to identify the assumptions implicit in a statement using a clear and exam-oriented approach for SSC Reasoning.

📘 What is Statement & Assumption?

In Statement & Assumption questions, a statement is given followed by one or more assumptions. Your task is to determine whether the given assumption is implicit in the statement.

Important: An assumption is an unstated idea, condition or belief that is taken for granted when making the statement.

These questions test your ability to identify what a speaker or writer is taking for granted while making a particular statement.

🔍 Statement vs Assumption

Statement: The information, opinion, suggestion or announcement given in the question.
Assumption: An unstated idea that is taken for granted while making the statement.

Example

Statement:

"Join our coaching programme to improve your performance in competitive examinations."

Assumption:

The coaching programme may help candidates improve their examination performance.

✓ Assumption is implicit.
The appeal relies on the idea that joining the programme can help improve performance.

🎯 Golden Rule of Statement & Assumption

Identify what must be taken for granted for the statement to make sense.
  • An assumption is not always directly stated.
  • It should be closely related to the given statement.
  • Do not treat every possible idea as an assumption.
  • Do not use outside knowledge unnecessarily.
  • Check whether the statement relies on the assumption.
  • Distinguish between an assumption and a conclusion.

📚 Important Types of Assumptions

1. Directly Related Assumption

An unstated idea closely connected with the purpose or meaning of the statement.

2. Purpose-Based Assumption

An idea taken for granted when a person makes a suggestion, advertisement or recommendation to achieve a particular purpose.

3. Need-Based Assumption

An assumption that a particular need or problem exists and that the proposed action may address it.

4. Audience-Based Assumption

An assumption about the people being addressed by an appeal, instruction or announcement.

Remember: An assumption must be implicit in the statement; it should not be an unrelated possibility.

🧠 How to Identify a Correct Assumption

1

Read the Statement

Understand the purpose, message or intention of the statement.

2

Read the Assumption

Identify the unstated idea presented in the assumption.

3

Find the Connection

Check whether the statement depends on that idea.

4

Check the Purpose

For advice or appeals, identify what the speaker expects or takes for granted.

5

Avoid Extreme Ideas

Be careful with assumptions containing unsupported words such as "all", "always" or "only".

6

Choose the Answer

Select the assumption that is implicit in the statement.

⚠️ Important Words to Watch

Certain words can change the meaning and strength of an assumption. Read them carefully.

  • All — refers to the complete group.
  • Some — refers to at least a part of a group.
  • Only — creates a specific restriction.
  • Always — makes a claim about every occasion.
  • Must — indicates necessity or obligation.
  • May — indicates possibility.
  • Because — may indicate a stated reason.
  • To / In order to — often indicates a purpose.
Exam Tip: Do not accept an assumption merely because it sounds reasonable. Check whether the statement actually relies on that idea.

🚀 Quick Solving Method

  1. Read the statement carefully.
  2. Identify its purpose, intention or message.
  3. Read each assumption separately.
  4. Check whether the statement relies on the assumption.
  5. Reject ideas that are unrelated or unnecessarily extreme.
  6. Do not confuse an assumption with a result or conclusion.
  7. Select the assumption that is implicit in the statement.
Fast Test:

Ask yourself:

"Is this idea being taken for granted when the statement is made?"

If the statement relies on the idea, the assumption may be implicit. If the idea is unrelated or merely possible, it may not be implicit.

❌ Common Mistakes

1. Treating Every Possibility as an Assumption

An idea may be possible without being necessary to the statement's meaning or purpose.

2. Confusing Assumption with Conclusion

An assumption is an idea taken for granted; a conclusion is an inference drawn from information.

3. Using Outside Knowledge

Focus on the statement and the relationship between the statement and the proposed assumption.

4. Ignoring the Purpose

For advice, instructions and advertisements, examine the purpose behind the statement.

5. Accepting Extreme Claims

Words such as all, always and only may make an assumption stronger than the statement supports.
