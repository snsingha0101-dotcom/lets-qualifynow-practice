Statement & Assumption

Learn how to identify the ideas or conditions that are implicitly accepted in a statement using a clear and exam-oriented approach.

📘 What is Statement & Assumption?

In Statement & Assumption questions, a statement is given followed by one or more assumptions. You have to determine whether the given assumption is implicitly taken for granted in the statement.

Important: An assumption is not simply a conclusion or a fact directly stated in the statement. It is an idea that is understood or accepted implicitly so that the statement makes sense.

These questions test your ability to identify the hidden idea, condition or belief on which a statement depends.

🔍 Statement vs Assumption

Statement:
The information, suggestion, announcement, advice or opinion given in the question.
Assumption:
An unstated idea that is taken for granted when the statement is made.
Key Difference:
A statement tells us something. An assumption is an idea that the speaker expects us to accept without directly saying it.

🎯 Core Concept

To identify an assumption, ask:

"What must be accepted or believed for this statement, suggestion or proposal to make sense?"

If the idea is necessary for the statement to work, it may be a valid assumption.

If the idea is merely possible, related, desirable or a personal opinion, it should not automatically be treated as an assumption.

🧠 The Negation Test

One useful method for checking an assumption is the Negation Test.

1

Read the Statement

Understand what the speaker is saying, suggesting or proposing.

2

Read the Assumption

Identify the hidden idea suggested by the assumption.

3

Negate the Assumption

Imagine that the assumption is false or not accepted.

4

Check the Statement

Ask whether the original statement still makes reasonable sense.

Exam Tip:
If rejecting the assumption makes the statement or proposal lose its basic purpose or meaning, the assumption is more likely to be implicit.

📚 Important Types of Assumptions

1. Purpose-Based Assumption

The assumption supports the purpose behind an action or suggestion.

2. Need-Based Assumption

The statement assumes that a particular need or problem exists.

3. Feasibility-Based Assumption

The statement may assume that the proposed action can actually be carried out.

4. Audience-Based Assumption

An advertisement, request or suggestion may assume that the target audience is interested in the subject.

5. Problem-Based Assumption

A proposed solution may assume that the stated problem exists and needs attention.

✍️ SSC-Level Solved Examples

Example 1

Statement:

"Buy our new study planner to organize your daily preparation more effectively."

Assumption:

Candidates may want to organize their daily preparation.

✓ Implicit Assumption
The suggestion to buy the planner depends on the idea that organizing preparation is useful or desirable to the target users.
Example 2

Statement:

"Use this online practice platform to improve your reasoning speed."

Assumption:

Some users want to improve their reasoning speed.

✓ Implicit Assumption
The suggestion assumes that improving reasoning speed is a relevant goal for the intended users.
Example 3

Statement:

"The school should introduce additional practice sessions for students before the examination."

Assumption:

Additional practice may help students prepare for the examination.

✓ Implicit Assumption
The proposal relies on the idea that additional practice can contribute to examination preparation.
Example 4

Statement:

"The company should provide training to employees before introducing the new software."

Assumption:

Employees may need to understand the new software before using it effectively.

✓ Implicit Assumption
The recommendation for training depends on the idea that employees need suitable understanding of the new software.
Example 5

Statement:

"Please carry an umbrella because rain is expected today."

Assumption:

Carrying an umbrella can help protect a person from rain.

✓ Implicit Assumption
The advice to carry an umbrella depends on its usefulness in protecting against rain.
Example 6

Statement:

"The library should extend its working hours during examination weeks."

Assumption:

Students may need additional access to the library during examination preparation.

✓ Implicit Assumption
The recommendation is based on the idea that additional library access could benefit students during examination preparation.

🚫 What is NOT an Assumption?

Example

Statement:

"The company should launch the product next month to reach more customers."

Possible Assumption:

More customers are interested in the product.

✓ Could be implicit
The proposed launch is based on the objective of reaching customers, so customer interest/relevance is connected to the purpose of the proposal.

Another statement:

The company has exactly 500 employees.

✗ Not an assumption of the original statement.
This is an independent fact that was not required for the proposal to make sense.

🚀 How to Solve Statement & Assumption Questions

  1. Read the complete statement carefully.
  2. Identify whether it is an advice, proposal, request, warning or opinion.
  3. Read each assumption separately.
  4. Ask what idea the speaker is taking for granted.
  5. Use the negation test when necessary.
  6. Do not choose an assumption simply because it is related to the statement.
  7. Do not add outside information.
  8. Select only the assumption that is logically required or strongly implicit.
Fast Test:

Ask:

"If this assumption is false, does the statement or proposal lose its basic purpose or meaning?"

🔄 Assumption vs Conclusion

Conclusion:
Something that logically follows from the information given.
Assumption:
Something that is taken for granted while making the statement.
Remember:
A conclusion generally comes after considering the statement, while an assumption is an idea that is already implicitly accepted when the statement is made.

⚠️ Important Clues in Questions

Pay special attention when the statement contains words such as:

  • Should — often indicates a recommendation.
  • Must — indicates necessity.
  • Please — often indicates a request or advice.
  • Buy — may indicate an advertisement or suggestion.
  • Use — may indicate advice or recommendation.
  • Need — may indicate an underlying requirement.
  • To improve — often indicates a purpose.
  • To prevent — indicates an intended objective.
Exam Tip:
The purpose of the statement often helps you identify the assumption behind it.

❌ Common Mistakes

1. Confusing Assumption with Conclusion

An assumption is an unstated idea taken for granted; it is not simply a result of the statement.

2. Choosing a Related Statement

An idea can be related to the statement without being necessary for it.

3. Using Outside Knowledge

Judge the assumption from the context of the given statement, not from personal experience.

4. Ignoring the Purpose

In advice, proposals and advertisements, identify the purpose behind the statement.

5. Making Extreme Assumptions

Words such as "everyone", "always", "only" and "exactly" may make an assumption unnecessarily strong.
