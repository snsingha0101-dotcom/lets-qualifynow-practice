👨‍👩‍👧 Blood Relations

Complete concept-based preparation covering family relationships, generation, gender, family tree, coded blood relations and exam-oriented practice.

📘 What is Blood Relation?

Understand family relationships logically and solve relation-based questions step by step.

Blood Relation questions test your ability to identify the relationship between two or more persons from the information given in the question.
Example:

A is the father of B.
B is the sister of C.

Question: How is A related to C?

Answer: Father

2️⃣ Generation & Gender

Generation Levels

One generation above:
Father, Mother, Uncle, Aunt

Same generation:
Brother, Sister, Cousin

One generation below:
Son, Daughter, Nephew, Niece
💡 Gender is important in Blood Relation questions. If the gender of a person is not given, do not assume it.

3️⃣ Maternal & Paternal Relations

Maternal Side – Mother's Side

Mother's father → Maternal Grandfather
Mother's mother → Maternal Grandmother
Mother's brother → Maternal Uncle
Mother's sister → Maternal Aunt
Paternal Side – Father's Side

Father's father → Paternal Grandfather
Father's mother → Paternal Grandmother
Father's brother → Paternal Uncle
Father's sister → Paternal Aunt

4️⃣ Direct & Indirect Relations

Direct Relation

A is the father of B.

A → Father of B

Indirect Relation

A is the brother of B.
B is the mother of C.

Therefore:

A is the Maternal Uncle of C.
✅ In indirect relations, follow every relationship one by one.

🌳 Family Tree Method

When many persons are given in a question, make a simple family tree. This prevents confusion.

Example:

Ravi is the father of Mohan.
Mohan is the brother of Sita.
Sita is the mother of Karan.

Therefore Ravi is the grandfather of Karan.

👉 Pointing / Introduction Based Questions

In these questions, one person points to or introduces another person and gives a family relationship.

Example:

Pointing to a boy, Amit says:

"He is the son of my sister."

Question: How is the boy related to Amit?

Answer: Nephew

🔐 Coded Blood Relation

In coded Blood Relation questions, symbols are used between two persons to represent their relationship.

📌 Basic Format

The most important thing is to understand the direction of the relation.

A + B = A is Brother of B
A - B = A is Sister of B
A × B = A is Father of B
A ÷ B = A is Mother of B
⚠️ These are example coded relations for understanding the concept. In an actual exam, the question may define different symbols or different relationships.

➡️ Direction is Very Important

A + B = A is Brother of B

Here:
A → Brother → B

Therefore the relation is from A toward B.
B + A = B is Brother of A

This is different from:
A + B = A is Brother of B
💡 Never reverse the order of the persons.

🧠 Example 1

A + B = A is Brother of B
B × C = B is Father of C

Question: How is A related to C?
Step 1:
A + B
= A is Brother of B.

Step 2:
B × C
= B is Father of C.

Therefore:
A is Brother of C's Father.

Answer: Paternal Uncle

🔗 Long Coded Relation

A + B = A is Brother of B
B × C = B is Father of C
C ÷ D = C is Mother of D

Question: How is A related to D?
A + B
= A is Brother of B.

B × C
= B is Father of C.

C ÷ D
= C is Mother of D.

Therefore:

A is brother of D's maternal grandfather.

Answer: Maternal Grand-Uncle

🧩 How to Solve Coded Blood Relation

  1. Read the complete coding information carefully.
  2. Convert each coded expression into a complete sentence.
  3. Always read the relation from the first person toward the second person.
  4. Do not reverse the order of the persons.
  5. Connect the relations one by one.
  6. For long chains, draw a family tree.
  7. Finally identify the relation asked in the question.
Golden Rule:

A + B = A is Brother of B

This means the relation is always read as:

First Person → Relationship → Second Person

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • ❌ Reading A + B as B is Brother of A.
  • ❌ Reversing the direction of the relation.
  • ❌ Treating a symbol as a universal relation.
  • ❌ Ignoring the complete expression.
  • ❌ Solving a long chain without converting it into sentences.
  • ❌ Assuming the gender of a person when it is not given.
