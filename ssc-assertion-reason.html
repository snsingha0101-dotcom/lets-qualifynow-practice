Assertion & Reason

Learn how to identify an assertion, evaluate its truth and determine whether the given reason correctly explains the assertion.

📘 What is Assertion & Reason?

Assertion & Reason questions contain two statements: an Assertion (A) and a Reason (R).

You have to determine whether the Assertion is true, whether the Reason is true, and whether the Reason correctly explains the Assertion.

Basic Concept:

Assertion → Is it True?
Reason → Is it True?
Then → Does Reason Explain Assertion?

🔍 Assertion vs Reason

Assertion (A)

The Assertion is the main statement or claim given in the question.

Reason (R)

The Reason is the statement provided to explain why the Assertion is true.

Important:
Even if both Assertion and Reason are true, the Reason may not necessarily be the correct explanation of the Assertion.

📋 Standard Answer Pattern

Assertion & Reason questions commonly use four answer choices based on the truth and relationship of the two statements.

Option A: Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
Option B: Both Assertion and Reason are true, but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion.
Option C: Assertion is true, but Reason is false.
Option D: Assertion is false, but Reason is true.
Note: Always read the exact answer choices given in the examination, because wording or option order may vary.

⭐ Golden Rule

First check the truth of Assertion and Reason separately. Only after that check the explanation relationship.
  1. Check whether Assertion is true or false.
  2. Check whether Reason is true or false.
  3. If both are true, ask whether Reason logically explains Assertion.
  4. Choose the answer according to the exact options.

🧠 How to Check Whether Reason Explains Assertion

Do not simply check whether the two statements are related. The Reason must provide a logical explanation for the Assertion.

1

Check Assertion

Determine whether the main statement is factually correct according to the information given.

2

Check Reason

Determine whether the Reason itself is true.

3

Ask Why?

Ask whether the Reason gives the actual logical cause or explanation for the Assertion.

4

Match the Option

Select the answer that matches the truth and relationship of A and R.

✍️ Solved Examples

Example 1 — Both True and Explanation

Assertion (A): Plants require sunlight for photosynthesis.

Reason (R): Sunlight provides energy required for photosynthesis.

Analysis: Both statements are true, and the Reason explains why sunlight is required for photosynthesis.

Correct Category: Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A.
Example 2 — Both True but Not Explanation

Assertion (A): The Earth revolves around the Sun.

Reason (R): The Earth has a natural satellite called the Moon.

Analysis: Both statements are true, but the presence of the Moon does not explain why the Earth revolves around the Sun.

Correct Category: Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
Example 3 — Assertion True, Reason False

Assertion (A): The Sun is a star.

Reason (R): The Sun is a planet of the Solar System.

Analysis: The Assertion is true, but the Reason is false.

Correct Category: A is true, but R is false.
Example 4 — Assertion False, Reason True

Assertion (A): The Moon produces its own light.

Reason (R): The Moon reflects sunlight.

Analysis: The Assertion is false, while the Reason is true.

Correct Category: A is false, but R is true.
Example 5 — Both True and Causal Explanation

Assertion (A): Metals generally expand when heated.

Reason (R): Heating increases the average separation between particles in the metal.

Analysis: Both statements are true, and the Reason provides the physical explanation for the expansion.
Example 6 — Related Does Not Always Mean Explanation

Assertion (A): Regular exercise can improve physical fitness.

Reason (R): Many people prefer exercising in the morning.

Analysis: The Assertion can be true, but the Reason does not explain why exercise improves physical fitness.

⚠️ Important Distinction: Truth vs Explanation

This is one of the most important points in Assertion & Reason questions.

Case 1: Both statements are true + Reason explains Assertion
Case 2: Both statements are true + Reason does not explain Assertion
Case 3: Assertion true + Reason false
Case 4: Assertion false + Reason true
Remember:
"Both are true" and "Reason explains Assertion" are two different checks.

🔗 Cause-Explanation Test

When both statements are true, mentally connect them using:

"Assertion is true because Reason."

If the sentence forms a logical explanation, the Reason may be the correct explanation.

If the two statements are merely related but the Reason does not explain the Assertion, they should not be treated as an explanatory pair.

📝 Step-by-Step SSC Method

  1. Read Assertion without being influenced by the Reason.
  2. Decide whether the Assertion is true or false.
  3. Read the Reason separately.
  4. Decide whether the Reason is true or false.
  5. If both are true, check the logical explanation relationship.
  6. Match the result with the answer choices.
Fast Method:

A → True/False   |   R → True/False   |   R explains A?

❌ Common Mistakes

1. Checking Only Whether Both Are True

Even if both statements are true, you must check whether the Reason explains the Assertion.

2. Assuming Any Relation Is an Explanation

Two statements can be related without one explaining the other.

3. Reading Both Statements as One Sentence

First evaluate Assertion and Reason separately.

4. Ignoring the Exact Answer Choices

Always match your analysis with the options given in the actual question.

5. Using Personal Belief

Use established facts and the information relevant to the question rather than personal assumptions.
